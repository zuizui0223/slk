## Candidate text

One prospectively registered system illustrates how G1-G5 can be operationalized without treating prior natural-history evidence as validation of the hierarchy. In *Pedicularis rex*, corolla exsertion above the cupulate bract is a candidate shared coordinate: prior field studies report positive associations of exsertion with pollination-related performance and with predispersal seed predation, while an independent drainage experiment establishes that water retained by the cupulate bracts has a defensive function (Sun & Huang 2015; Sun, Armbruster & Huang 2016). Our registered test therefore keeps water defence fixed while first identifying the shared-coordinate conflict and `L` from a crossed exsertion x pollination-dependence x predator-exposure design. A separate structural-retention axis `y` is then required to pass repeatability, preferential-loading, and intervention gates before an externalized retention comparator can be used as the pre-cost `D0` state. In the final independent experiment, shared (`S`), pre-cost differentiated (`D0`), and realized differentiated (`D`) worlds are each re-optimized over the same preregistered exsertion grid and the same reproductive-fitness scale, giving `R=W_D0,pre* - W_S*`, `K=W_D0,pre* - W_D*`, and `Phi=W_D* - W_S*`. This is a prospective measurement programme, not a completed empirical test: no *P. rex* G1-G5 receipt is claimed in the present paper.
