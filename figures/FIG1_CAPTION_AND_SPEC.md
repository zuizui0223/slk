# Figure 1 — one persistent phenotype, three evolutionary bottlenecks

## Caption

**Figure 1. One persistent phenotype can hide different evolutionary bottlenecks.** The diagram contrasts three reasons why one specified completed divided alternative may fail to replace an integrated architecture: negative endpoint value (`Phi<0`), an unfavorable intrinsic local release path (`Phi>0`, `g0<0`), or negative rare endpoint performance (`Phi>0`, `g0>0`, `Delta_R<0`). This comparison is candidate-relative: endpoint exclusion does not prove persistence against accessible partial variants.

The figure links one visible resident architecture to different **candidate-relative decision barriers**; the link is conditional on accessible alternative forms and evolutionary history.

## Interpretation

The phenotype is many-to-one with respect to evolutionary state. The focal completed divided alternative can be less profitable, intrinsically path-inaccessible through small steps, or disadvantaged by rarity. None of these endpoint tests alone certifies global evolutionary stability of the integrated phenotype. Those states differ in how they should respond when architecture, developmental routes, or interacting communities change.

The route with `Phi>0, g0>0, Delta_R>0` removes the three early barriers represented in the figure but does not imply that differentiation must fix or persist historically.

## Scope

Finite-population fixation and weak-mutation occupancy remain valid downstream results in the theory, but they are intentionally omitted from the main causal branches because they are downstream events and do not replace testing the invasion performance of all accessible partial alternatives.
