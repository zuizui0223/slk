# From stamen loss to new floral function: a real biological bridge between SLK and ANTO

**21 October 2026 | SLK biological synthesis and independent original-source F2 reanalysis | review-stage draft, not an original organismal experiment or evidence of field evolutionary rescue**

## The central biological question

**After a reproductive organ loses its ancestral role, does the organism lose its capacity to reconstruct that structure, or can some developmental parts remain and acquire other functions under different pollinators?**

SLK asks when a shared structure should integrate, differentiate or reroute distinct ecological functions under real reproductive interactions. ANTO asks when the resources to express a visible form remain accessible after its historical loss. A homologue that *reappears* may be a sterile pollination lever rather than a recovered pollen-producing stamen. Morphological recovery, original-function recovery and new-function recruitment are thus NOT interchangeable outcomes.

The strongest previously overlooked natural biological bridge is **staminode evolution**. This is not a newly discovered concept: Walker-Larsen & Harder 2000–2001 studied staminode loss/reappearance and functional reinvention explicitly (see references). The novelty-to-this-research-program is an explicit, quantitatively auditable shared-organ bridge, not a claim of new broad evo-devo theory.

## 1. Published pollination manipulation: the same ancestral organ can acquire different ecological roles

**Walker-Larsen & Harder 2001**, *Evolution* 55:477–487, DOI [10.1111/j.0014-3820.2001.tb00782.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.0014-3820.2001.tb00782.x), compared flowers **with and without staminodes** in four *Penstemon* species, using appropriate pollination outcomes.

| Focal taxon and pollination system | Directly supported intervention outcome | Not established |
|---|---|---|
| *P. ellipticus*, bee | Staminode impedes nectar access; longer handling/contact raises stigmatic pollen receipt and regulates pollen removal | Does not establish genetic restoration of fertile pollen-producing organ or total lifetime fitness advantage |
| *P. palmeri*, bee | Staminode behaves as a lever that affects stigma contact; removal reduces pollen receipt without changing visit length/attraction or pollen removal | A distinct mechanism from the *P. ellipticus* nectar barrier; does not show exact same morphological mechanism |
| *P. centranthifolius*, hummingbird | Removal did not show an effect on the measured pollination components | Non-detection is not demonstration of zero evolutionary utility under all situations |
| *P. rostriflorus*, hummingbird | Same no-detected-effect outcome in the reported removal comparisons | Not a tested genomic erasure or present/future evolutionary impossibility |

The study links phylogenetic/developmental reasoning that a modified stamen may disappear visibly yet later reappear in the tribe Cheloneae. That is **historical inference**, not a modern sequencing test of intact ancestral genes in an initially staminode-lacking individual. The staminode remains **sterile**: floral pollination-assisting function may reappear or innovate without the ancestral role of making functional pollen. These four species are **one study**, not four independent genetic restorations.

**Independent additional ecological contrast:** Ortega-Olivencia and collaborators 2016, *International Journal of Plant Sciences* 177:671–681, DOI [10.1086/687994](https://doi.org/10.1086/687994), experimentally removed staminodes from two species with large staminodes, *Scrophularia lyrata* and *S. scorodonia*, and one small-staminode species, *S. canina*. The larger staminodes increased attraction/reward access and repeatedly visited flowers showed reduced fruit/seed production upon removal, but this pattern was not detected in *S. canina*; approximately 10.5–20% of observed *S. canina* flowers normally lack staminodes. This is context-sensitive organ function and standing morphological absence, **not a within-species verified ancestral-regain genetic experiment**.

Together these observations show **real functional divergence in a homologous origin class of floral structures** (modified stamens), while preserving strong differences among species and experiments.

## 2. Original F2 source reanalysis: the stamen–staminode boundary is a mosaic, not simply present/absent

**Johns et al. 2024**, *EvoDevo* 15:6, DOI [10.1186/s13227-024-00225-3](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13227-024-00225-3), studied *Aquilegia jonesii*, in which the two normally staminodial whorls have reverted to stamens, versus *A. coerulea* ‘Origami’, which forms a staminode sheath. Their F2 experiment genotyped a larger collection; our trait analysis specifically uses the **original phenotyped subset** of 231 F2 plants, 400 flowers and two whorls per flower. Source is the author's [Supplementary Material 3 CSV](https://static-content.springer.com/esm/art%3A10.1186%2Fs13227-024-00225-3/MediaObjects/13227_2024_225_MOESM3_ESM.csv) (published CC BY 4.0), preserved under `data/external/johns2024_aquilegia_f2_phenotypes_source.csv` (verified Git blob SHA1 `9911d4a5e85fa8629ad9890f28992e0f07e5feca`).

The original authors reported **9 independent QTL and 44–53% explained trait variation**, supporting polygenic control with shared and whorl-specific regions. Those are ORIGINAL AUTHORS' QTL results, **not a refitted QTL model using the CSV**, since this CSV does not include marker genotypes.

We independently parsed and counted the actual morphologies with BOTH whorls paired within the SAME flower. Traits:
- `AN=1`: **presence of an anther**, NOT confirmed pollen viability or export
- `LE=0..3`: degree of filament lateral expansion, NOT a direct mechanical support performance measure
- `FU=1`: neighboring filament fusion **between whorls**; source CSV repeats the same flower-level FU score on both whorl rows. Therefore 800 rows do NOT constitute 800 independent fusion measurements.

### Direct biological source counts, with appropriate nesting

| Readout | Actual F2 source result |
|---|---:|
| Phenotyped F2 plants / flowers / whorl score rows | **231 / 400 / 800** |
| Parent plants represented by one vs two measured flowers | **62 / 169** |
| Pure staminode score (AN0, LE3, FU1) | **240 / 800** whorl scores |
| Pure stamen score (AN1, LE0, FU0) | **119 / 800** whorl scores |
| All other phenotypic combinations | **441 / 800 (55.1%)** whorl scores |
| AN1 with strongly laterally expanded (LE≥2), fused (FU1) filaments | **187 / 800 (23.4%)** whorl scores, **129 / 400 flowers** and **91 / 231 plants** with ≥1 matching whorl |
| Same flower has anther in outer but absent in inner whorl | **99 / 400 flowers (24.8%)** |
| Same flower has anther in inner but absent in outer whorl | **0 / 400** |
| Inner filament expansion greater than outer | **181 / 400** |
| Outer expansion greater than inner | **2 / 400** (plants 132-a and 39-a, from the FULL 400-flower enumeration) |
| Fusion-positive flowers | **234 / 400 flowers**, not 468 separate organ-fusion experiments |

Crucial nuance: the published authors compared whorls using **one randomly chosen flower per plant** and reported 28% anther discordance and a consistent inner-greater staminodial direction. We included BOTH measured flowers from 169 plants, yielding 99/400 anther discordances and two outer-greater LE flowers. These are **different denominators/sampling schemes**, not a repudiation of the original result. Fusion is recorded across whorls and is not evidence of independently gained fusion in two structures.

### What the original-source combination really indicates

**Supported:** Developmental characteristics of anther formation, filament expansion, fusion, and inner/outer identity can coexist in forms intermediate between the two parental endpoints. The anther/filament identity boundary is ordered directionally in the vast majority of measured flowers, rather than a fully unconstrained randomly permutable LEGO assembly. In particular, **anther identity is not sufficient to predict the entire surrounding sterile-like morphology**.

**Not supported:** a proof that the three phenotypic scores correspond to three independently segregating functional genes, that anther-positive organs make viable pollen, that chimeras transmit pollen better, that any F2 phenotype is advantageous with a particular pollinator, or that a naturally lost structure spontaneously re-evolved in this hybrid garden. No ancestral/derived chromosome genealogy or transition rate is reconstructed from this file. The 400 flowers are repeated observations on **231 F2 individuals**, and plant-level evidence must not use flower rows as independent evolutionary replicates.

The broad conclusion that staminode loss is incomplete and polygenic was already made by the 2024 authors. This analysis contributes a precisely counted **within-flower source bridge** with explicit grain, and should not be presented as a new original evolutionary discovery.

## 3. The SLK × ANTO connection and a genuinely new testable prediction

These independent studies occupy complementary, NOT jointly measured, links:

| Biological step | Actual positive evidence | Still missing |
|---|---|---|
| Ancestral pollen-organ identity can be repurposed | *Penstemon* staminodes are sterile but pollination-assisting; phylogenetic support for staminode disappearance/reappearance in Cheloneae | Contemporary native-locus functional retention/regain in the same lineage |
| Developmental organ subtraits can reorganize incompletely | *Aquilegia* original F2 morphologies, paired inner/outer whorls; authors' polygenic QTL results | Evolutionary restoration route in uncrossed wild population; actual fitness of mosaics |
| Organ function depends on partner guild and visit sequence | Bee versus hummingbird *Penstemon* removal effects; *Scrophularia* repeated-visit reproductive effects | Same-genotype pollinator-crossover experiment linked to molecular developmental capacity |
| SLK alternative-structure value and invasion | Published flower-manipulation outcomes partially identify pollen receipt/routing | Relative **lifetime** architecture fitness, candidate S/P/D, rare-variant dynamics |
| ANTO trait afterlife | Author-inferred retained organ-program potential and F2 hybrid phenotypic accessibility | Native lineage-level retained functional modules, heritable re-expression after documented prior loss |

The **narrow falsifiable prediction** to pursue is not “vestigial organs can have new functions”—this has been known for decades. Instead:

> **When developmental organ identity is rebuilt piecemeal, a structurally intermediate phenotype can preserve a pollinator-handling function without restoring the ancestral pollen-production role; the net benefit of preserving or repurposing that structure should change with measured pollinator identity and floral developmental background.**

To falsify: independently manipulate the same organ's **fertility** (where biologically feasible), structural lever/barrier position, and contact geometry within the same lineage; measure stigma deposition and downstream mature viable seed/paternity for multiple pollinator guilds; genotype and developmental-assay the very same plants including parent–offspring lines; compare controlled morphological mosaics with both parental endpoints. A null or negative role for genuine intermediates across matched guilds, or complete inability of within-lineage native genetic resources to rebuild the candidate, would reject important aspects. Beware artificial F2 linkage, genotype-by-environment context, allometry and removal wounding.

Among existing sources, **Aquilegia** now supplies real within-plant architecture data with genotype/QTL context but not pollinator/payoff data. **Penstemon** supplies the ecological manipulation precedent, but not the native genetic restoration data. **Scrophularia canina** offers standing organ presence/absence in one species, but heritability and direct ecological payoff remain unresolved. Current *Cirsium* datasets have real insects, head seeds and Ryukyu young-leaf RNA but no single-individual genetic-structure×pollination-fitness join; aza3 exclusively owns those empirical datasets. It would be scientifically incorrect to pool these as a single “observed 3-gate” event or as three new independent replications of an SLK threshold.

## 4. Research and repository decision

- Keep the SLK theory flagship and main submission unchanged.
- Hold the original-source cross-system morphology + ecology narrative on this daughter draft PR (child of [SLK biological ANTO bridge #119](https://github.com/zuizui0223/slk/pull/119)).
- General ANTO remains evolutionary-availability theory only; cite these real comparative anchors, do not duplicate Aquilegia empirical scripts into ANTO. All Cirsium empirical work stays in [aza3](https://github.com/zuizui0223/aza3).
- Before proposing a new paper, require a real **same-organ, same-lineage** contrast of pollen function, other function, developmental capacity and total reproductive outcome, so the result can go beyond Walker-Larsen & Harder 2001's already-established functional reinvention.
- For quantitative cross-angiosperm generality, a future database must count phylogenetically independent native loss/re-expression origins and functional routes, not treat 400 F2 flowers, multiple study outcomes or separate taxa as 400 independent origins.
