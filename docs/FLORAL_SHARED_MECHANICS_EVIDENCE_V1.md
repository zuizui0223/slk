# SLK organismal evidence — pollen partition and mechanical coupling can coexist

## Biological question

**Can flowers differentiate the roles of their stamens while still relying on physical interactions among stamens, petals, and pollinators to deliver pollen?**

Yes, for *specific components of floral performance*, already published manipulation experiments directly demonstrate cross-organ mechanical routes even in flowers with specialized morphology. Whether such coupling **evolutionarily maintains an incompletely divided architecture** after changes in the visitor community remains open. This note separates that positive observed result from the still-untested selection/history interpretation; it does not invent a new "fourth SLK bottleneck."

The term *mechanical coupling* refers here to physical vibration transfer, bee support/gripping or trajectory redirection. It is **not** synonymous with a lack of anatomical differentiation or the absence of pollen-related specialization.

## Three strong intervention anchors, each resolving a different biological link

### 1. Joined anther cones transmit vibrations, but do not always release more pollen

Vallejo-Marín, Pereira Nunes & Russell (2022), *Evolution* 76:931–945, DOI **10.1111/evo.14485**, used artificial bee-like vibrations and experimentally created joined versus free anther cones across **three** *Solanum* species.

- In all three species, artificial joining **increased vibration velocity at nonfocal anthers**: a direct mechanical consequence of inter-anther coupling.
- Joining also increased **pollen release** in *S. elaeagnifolium* and *S. sisymbriifolium*, the species with relatively loose natural anther arrangements (on average approximately **1,500–2,300 additional pollen grains** in the published comparisons, with species-specific mixed-model estimates).
- In *S. pyracanthos*, where anthers are already naturally tightly positioned, joining raised transmitted vibration but **did not significantly increase pollen release** (reported marginal-mean contrast `P=0.169`). Thus *mechanical transmission* and *amount of pollen removed* cannot be treated as the same response; a downstream release bottleneck (e.g., pore size/available pollen) is plausible but was not isolated experimentally as the explanation.
- The experiment did **not** estimate transfer to conspecific stigmas, seed production, outcross paternity, or selection maintaining anther fusion. Pollen **release** is not yet reproductive pollen **export**.

Raw-data and source audit trail: Dryad DOI **10.5061/dryad.s1rn8pk9p** contains `mdat_20220314.csv` and `pollen_20220314.csv`, besides a large vibration archive. The present SLK audit uses **published paper results**; the raw CSV was **not independently retrieved/reanalysed** in this update. Do not relabel the summarized comparisons as a new independent data analysis.

### 2. A modified petal redirects pollen from both anther classes toward a stigma-facing region

Monteiro and colleagues (published online 2024; *Annals of Botany* **135:669–680**, 2025 issue), DOI **10.1093/aob/mcae210**, manipulated the cucculus (specialized petal) and anther pores in *Chamaecrista latistipula* using a **2 × 3 arrangement** (cucculus normal versus deflected; both, only small, or only large anthers releasing). Flowers were artificially sonicated with a **3-D-printed bee mimic**, not freely behaving bees; fresh flowers were assigned to treatments.

- With the cucculus intact, pollen density on the artificial bee's adjacent lateral, **stigma-contact-facing** region was **9.88 times** that with the petal deflected; density on the ventral region was **2.31 times** higher (published model contrasts). These are **pollen-covered image-area density contrasts**, not 9.88-fold or 2.31-fold increases in male fitness.
- In the intact-petal treatment, the adjacent lateral density when **only small anthers** could release pollen was **1.59 times** that when **only large anthers** could release. Both anther classes can therefore contribute pollen to a nominal reproductive contact zone. However, small anthers also contribute most of the flower's pollen supply (authors note approximately 64%, citing unpublished counts), so the 1.59 ratio **does not demonstrate greater per-grain reproductive efficiency** of small anthers.
- The **mean distance** of pollen placement from the stigma position was **not significantly shifted** by the petal or anther-class manipulation. The petal had a comparatively **small effect (~10%) on placement imprecision**. It is therefore misleading to call the approximately tenfold **density** increase a tenfold **accuracy** improvement.
- The paper conducted its principal pollen-density analyses on selected treatment subsets rather than one omnibus six-condition interaction test; do **not** infer a proven superadditive petal × anther synergy from the title alone.
- Artificial bee body, static fluorescence indicating stigma-contact region, and pollen-covered pixels cannot establish actual pollinator grooming, outcross pollen fate, reproductive selection, or population maintenance.

**Biological interpretation:** anther identity does not uniquely determine pollen destination. An auxiliary flower part can alter *where* pollen from either morph is placed. Apparent feeding/pollinating roles are conditional on the entire floral contact apparatus and the interacting pollinator, not a fixed property of each anther alone.

### 3. Stamen filament support changes live-bee posture, not lifetime fitness

Fukano & Denda (2026), *Plant Species Biology* **41:e70070**, DOI **10.1111/1442-1984.70070**, performed selected component-removal contrasts on live *Bredia hirsuta* visitors. With short stamens retained and long-stamen anthers removed in both arms, retaining **long-stamen filaments** (treatment D) yielded **0/31 slipping events** and **0/31 ventral stigma contacts**; with filaments also removed (treatment B), these were **12/39** and **13/39**, respectively.

This supports the role of long-stamen **filaments**, independently of long anther pollen release, in shaping *buzzing posture*. It does **not** prove that both anther classes must always be mechanically present: long stamens alone (treatment C) also had **0/26 slips**. Counts refer to repeated **visits**, not independent flowers and not lifetime seed production. See `docs/FLORAL_INTERACTION_MODULE_AUDIT_V1.md` for the complete six-treatment evidence table.

## Independent pollen-fate assay — architecture changes where pollen is lost, not demonstrably how much reaches stigmas

Vasquez-Castro, Morel, Garcia-Simpson & Vallejo-Marín (2025), *Journal of Pollination Ecology* 39:246–260, DOI **10.26786/1920-7603(2025)836**, directly tracked the fate of pollen removed by captive *Bombus terrestris* from **two different species**: heterantherous, enantiostylous *Solanum rostratum* and *S. dulcamara*, with morphologically uniform anthers joined into a cone. Each controlled trial exposed a donor to the bee and then **four recipient stigmas**, with recipients' anther pores blocked. There were **six** completed trials for *S. rostratum* and **nine** for *S. dulcamara* (**15 donor trials**, 60 sequential recipient flowers). The 60 recipients are repeated observations nested within 15 trials, not 60 independent architecture comparisons.

| Outcome as percentage of pollen removed from the donor | *S. rostratum* (heteranthery) | *S. dulcamara* (joined anther cone) |
|---|---:|---:|
| Pollen dislodged onto collection plate/ground | **62.99%** | **19.07%** |
| Pollen in bee corbiculae (harvested by bee) | **27.65%** | **65.92%** |
| Pollen still on bee body at the end of the trial | 8.54% | 14.01% |
| Pollen deposited on four recipient stigmas | approximately **1%** | approximately **1%** |

Ground-loss and corbicular differences were statistically detectable (reported `P=0.001` and `P=0.011`, respectively). The difference in stigma-deposition efficiency (PTE, fraction of *removed* pollen reaching the four measured stigmas) was **not statistically detected** (`P=0.221`), **not an equivalence test**. The abstract and discussion report `0.86% S. rostratum / 1.00% S. dulcamara`; the species order preceding these numbers in a Results sentence differs. We therefore use **approximately 1%** for both in the biological comparison rather than treating this textual inconsistency as a resolved source-level species difference. The four-recipient design truncates carryover: extending a fitted curve to 20 recipients was projected to increase cumulative PTE by roughly **25.7%** relative to that window; this was a model extrapolation, not 20-visit experimental observation.

**Ecological implication:** similar measured stigma-transfer fractions can conceal distinct **pollen loss pathways**. Pollen spilled onto the ground does not reward the visitor, whereas pollen groomed into corbiculae is captured by the bee. The two routes differ in plant–pollinator resource economics even when the short-range measured export fraction is similar. A flower with apparently clearer anther-role division is *not necessarily more efficient at overall pollen transfer under every visitor*; the visitor's grip, size, and handling can alter the balance among pollen harvest, waste, and available pollen for transfer. This is a *synthesis of published data*, not evidence that floral coupling, structural specialization, or pollinator reward was evolutionarily selected or caused differences in future visitation.

**Hard limits:** The two species are not matched derived alternatives within one lineage; anatomy, flower size, bee-fit, pollen supply, and mating system differ. This experiment manipulates **pollen pathway/recipient accessibility**, not a free-versus-differentiated anther genotype; it does **not** assign each lost grain to a particular anther class, measure seed fitness or paternity, or estimate `Phi`, `g0` or rare invasion `Delta_R`. Bee grooming or pollen transport after departing the donor was incompletely measured. The confined arena, artificial flower orientation and use of one captive bee species cannot establish field population selection, nor does failure to detect a PTE difference demonstrate that the two architectures are equivalent.

Source: Vasquez-Castro CA, Morel E, Garcia-Simpson B, Vallejo-Marín M. 2025. The fate of pollen in two morphologically contrasting buzz-pollinated *Solanum* flowers. *Journal of Pollination Ecology* 39:246–260. DOI 10.26786/1920-7603(2025)836.

## Within-species bee–flower fit: more received pollen need not mean more mature seeds

Solís-Montero & Vallejo-Marín (2017), *Ecology and Evolution* 7:2706–2715, DOI **10.1002/ece3.2897**, examined pollinator-size matching **within the heterantherous species *Solanum rostratum*** rather than treating different species as randomized architectural alternatives. They surveyed variation in floral size/herkogamy in six native Mexican populations, then exposed controlled arrays (35 blocks, ten potted plants per array) to captive *Bombus terrestris* workers of varying size. Each array contained flowers from two populations and was visited by one bee during a 20-minute exposure; multiple flowers/visits are nested observations, not independent evolutionary replicates.

The declared **size-matching index** was separation between the pollinating anther and stigma **minus** bee abdomen width (`SMI = DPAST - BAW`). Negative values mean the bee abdomen is wider than the gap between sexual organs; positive values mean it is narrower.

| Biological response in *S. rostratum* | Reported SMI coefficient | Evidence | Ecological meaning |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Pollen grains deposited on recipient stigmas | **−0.068** | `P<0.001` | A wider bee abdomen relative to the organ gap predicts **more pollen receipt** |
| Probability of fruit production | −0.186 | `P=0.094` | Directionally similar to receipt, but **not statistically detected** |
| Seeds per fruit | **+0.214** | `P<0.001` | The **opposite** direction: narrower bee abdomen relative to the gap predicts **more mature seeds per fruit** |

This is a strong natural-organism analogy for **stage-dependent reproductive consequences of floral architecture × pollinator morphology**: pollen deposition is not a monotone substitute for mature seed yield. The authors offered mechanistic possibilities (excess pollen clogging a small stigma, or deposition of geitonogamous self-pollen followed by inbreeding depression). The experiment did **not** determine the self:outcross pollen ratio, pollen-donor paternity, male function or the causal postpollination mechanism. Seed number per fruit is not necessarily total lifetime female fitness, especially when fruit production itself did not change significantly.

The 2017 within-species bee-fit result and the 2025 two-species pollen-fate comparison concern **different experiments and reproductive stages**. Read together, they show why an apparent advantage at pollen release, transport, stigma arrival, fruit initiation or mature seed production cannot automatically be moved to another endpoint or interpreted as selection preserving a particular architectural module. Both are **prior published biology**, not a newly demonstrated SLK `Phi`, `g0`, `Delta_R` crossing or natural evolutionary transition.

Source: Solís-Montero L, Vallejo-Marín M. 2017. Does the morphological fit between flowers and pollinators affect pollen deposition? An experimental test in a buzz-pollinated species with anther dimorphism. *Ecology and Evolution* 7:2706–2715. DOI 10.1002/ece3.2897.

## The shared positive ecological conclusion — and its limit

The three studies establish distinct real effects of **organ-to-organ or plant-to-pollinator coupling**:

| Mechanism | Directly manipulated | Directly measured | Not resolved |
|---|---|---|---|
| Anther–anther vibration transfer | Joined/free anthers in three *Solanum* | Nonfocal vibration; pollen release | Reproductive export, lifetime fitness, persistence of coupling |
| Petal–anther pollen routing | Cucculus geometry; open anther classes in *Chamaecrista* | Artificial-bee pollen density by body region; placement imprecision | Real-bee grooming, paternity, net selection on anatomy |
| Stamen–bee mechanical grip | Long filaments versus removal in *Bredia* | Slip behavior and stigma-contact posture | Male-fitness gains, evolutionary maintenance of dimorphism |

**Supported synthesis:** differentiation of pollen source and physical integration of its transport or handling route can be present in the same floral organization. Stronger coupling can raise vibration transmission while adding **no** pollen release, and a feeding-stamen class can still contribute pollen to a stigma-contact region. These observations challenge a one-organ/one-output interpretation of structural division, **not** the existence or general value of division of labour.

**Not supported:** ecological coupling invariably maintains a dimorphic architecture; mechanical linkage is always adaptive; removal of the petal necessarily reduces paternity 9.88-fold; the published experiments determine SLK's intrinsic value `Phi`, local accessibility `g0`, or rare architectural invasion `Delta_R`. There is no demonstration of an ordered three-barrier turnover in a natural lineage.

## Mechanical usefulness may select for the scaffold, not the differentiated pollen source

This is the strongest caution from bringing the new studies together. **A useful supporting module is not proof that a pollen-bearing specialized anther must be retained.**

In *Bredia*, long filaments with long anthers removed help stabilize live-bee posture relative to removing both, so the **support structure** may be functionally useful without the **long-anther pollen source**. In *Chamaecrista*, the cucculus can route pollen from either anther class to the bee's stigma-contact side, so the **pollen transport route** is not intrinsically owned by a dedicated pollinating anther type. In *Solanum*, increased vibrational coupling is not enough to guarantee increased pollen release, even before asking about successful export.

Therefore two distinct evolutionary possibilities must remain in the candidate set: (i) mechanical coupling genuinely complements differentiated anthers, and keeping both is favored; or (ii) the plant preserves a filament or petal-based **scaffold while reducing the specialization of pollen-producing anther tissue**. The latter is a possible anatomical *bypass* of an assumption that full dimorphism is the only way to keep the mechanical benefit.

A separate conditional fitness comparison could distinguish full heteranthery from an architecture retaining a support scaffold but not differentiated pollen-bearing anthers. This evidence audit identifies **empirical mechanisms** motivating that counterfactual but supplies neither its complete-cycle fitness comparison nor a demonstrated evolutionary transition. None of the cited studies follows the ancestral-to-descendant structural change while measuring reproductive fitness of both architectures.

## A discriminating ecological hypothesis for incomplete division of labour

Treat **pollen-role partition** and **mechanical coupling** as different biological axes, not opposite ends of the same scalar. For the same flower and pollinator community, three observables have different meanings:

1. **Source partition:** reproductive destination of pollen from small versus large stamens (needs source labeling/transfer to other flowers, not merely stamen size).
2. **Interaction coupling:** transmitted buzz velocity, filaments supporting grip, or a petal routing grains to stigma-contact regions (already directly demonstrable).
3. **Net architecture value:** total male + female reproductive performance of the actual differentiated architecture against an achievable less differentiated alternative, including assembly and maintenance costs (not available from these studies).

The *proposed* ecological prediction is that, as pollinator identity/size/behavior changes, the **fitness benefit of source partition can decline independently of the fitness benefit of mechanical coupling**. A flower could then retain differentiated stamens for a secondary function, or favor reducing the difference between stamens while retaining the coupling structure. These evolutionary outcomes are competing possibilities: **no current field series demonstrates the historical retention mechanism**.

Crucially, the *Chamaecrista* and *Solanum* experiments already establish that a **mechanical effect need not map one-to-one onto pollen fate**, so no universal monotone mapping from more structural differentiation to more exported pollen follows. The strictly convex recovery `R(d)` of the canonical SLK atlas must remain a **declared conditional shape assumption**, not an empirical general law for heterantherous flowers.

### What would count as a decisive biological observation?

In one natural population system where the pollinator community varies or changes, retain or experimentally alter the floral mechanical route while preserving pollen source and amount, and independently modify anther-class morphology/pollen release. Measure where pollen from each class reaches real receivers and the plant's male and female reproductive output. A **persisting** differentiated architecture attributed to secondary mechanical benefit additionally requires ancestral/history evidence that the anatomy predated the partner shift and a demonstrable fitness advantage relative to a feasible reduced-dimorphism alternative; a de novo dimorphism origin is not equivalent.

This is a direction for an evolutionary ecology investigation, not an assertion that the cited experiments already supplied the needed evolutionary comparison.
