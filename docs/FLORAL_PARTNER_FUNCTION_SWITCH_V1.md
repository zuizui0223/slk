# SLK empirical extension — pollinator-dependent function of differentiated stamens

## Ecological question

**Can a structurally divided floral organ remain differentiated after the ecological partner no longer uses its originally hypothesized roles — and could a second function retain that anatomy?**

This is a **proposed natural-history hypothesis**, not an observed evolutionary transition and not a new theorem. It asks whether a pollinator change can decouple (i) morphological stamen dimorphism, (ii) actual pollen-reward versus pollen-transfer specialization, and (iii) support of pollinator posture. These are different biological outcomes, not synonymous proxies for selection or fitness.

### Evidence with explicitly bounded roles

| Natural system | Established observations | Supported inference | Not established |
|---|---|---|---|
| *Solanum rostratum* — Vallejo-Marín et al. 2009 | Bumble bees preferentially manipulate feeding stamens, whereas pollen from pollinating stamens is exported more to other flowers | Direct functional division for pollen food versus transfer under studied pollinator behavior | A general fitness advantage of all heteranthery configurations under every pollinator |
| *Senna arnottiana* — Rego, Monzón & Mesquita-Neto 2024 | In three populations, large native bees preferentially visit short stamens; introduced *Bombus terrestris* sonicates/forages on short and long stamens without the same distinction and contacts stigmas less often | Direct ecological **loss of handling selectivity** and lower stigma-contact propensity in the introduced visitor. Heteranthery's adaptive pollen allocation may be compromised | No demonstrated historical loss of heteranthery, no experimental evolution, no estimate of fitness of an engineered isomorphic alternative, no proof the floral phenotype persists because of support function |
| *Bredia hirsuta* — Fukano & Denda 2026 | Fieldwork in Okinawa (2022–2025), 193 h video. *Amegilla dulcifera* consistently grips short stamens ventrally while long-stamen tips and stigma face the dorsal abdomen. *Lasioglossum* visit posture varies and often removes stigma pollen. Targeted removals showed 12 slipping events in 39 visits with whole long stamens removed, compared with 0 in 33 control visits and 0 in 31 visits where long **anthers**, but not their filaments, were removed | Behavioral division of roles is plausible in small flowers. **Stamen filaments also mechanically stabilize the bee's buzz posture**, giving a second biologically important function to parts of differentiated organs | No measured evolutionary maintenance of dimorphism after partner turnover, no complete seed-fitness selection differential between a dimorphic and an isomorphic architecture; fluorescent stigma-contact powder is not a direct genetic paternity assay |
| Merianieae — Dellinger et al. 2021 | Across 63 species, anther dimorphism occurs or evolves in nectar/food-body rewarding lineages after pollen is no longer the reward; passerines remove anther types on different visits | Direct phylogenetic and behavioral precedent for **repurposed differentiated morphology** toward temporally staggered resource presentation | Does not show a single ancestral heteranthery architecture persisted unchanged after an observed ecological shift; repeated de novo origins are part of the authors' result |

## Three competing biological explanations for a retained stamen morphology

After pollinator change, suppose the large and small stamens are still visibly differentiated but pollen export between them becomes similar.

**H1: Functional release with a new supporting function (proposed).** Structural differentiation persists not because reward-versus-fertilization pollen specialization remains strong, but because stamen position/filament rigidity facilitates a reproducible pollinator grip, stigma contact, or another independent function. It predicts that targeted removal or altered stiffness of the supporting filaments impairs posture or realized pollen transfer **even after pollen-availability differences are experimentally controlled**. In repeated populations under different pollinator assemblages, this mechanical effect should predict relative reproductive success better than anther dimorphism alone. The 2026 *Bredia* evidence supports the existence of this mechanical role in one ecology; retention after a shift is not tested.

**H2: Ecological mismatch plus historical lag (proposed).** Morphology survives temporarily despite lower current fitness return, because the lineage has not experienced enough generations, mutational access is limited, or selection is weak. It predicts no independent mechanical benefit capable of offsetting the value lost from indiscriminate pollen collection, and potential subsequent erosion of stamen differentiation if these new environments persist. The 2024 *Senna* study demonstrates altered visitor handling but does not evaluate the transgenerational prediction.

**H3: Reassignment toward temporal presentation (already demonstrated in another lineage).** Dimorphism provides differential timing of pollen or food-body presentation rather than the ancestral pollen dilemma's division between reward and reproduction. The Merianieae study documents this mechanism as prior art. No first-discovery claim is warranted. It predicts contrasts in removal timing through anthesis and reproductive transfer benefits even when the visitor does not consume pollen.

These scenarios are **not mutually exclusive**: mechanical support, staggered presentation, reward differentiation, and developmental inertia can coexist. A flower may preserve the same visible organ categories while functions and selective consequences change, but the proposed persistence mechanism needs a *candidate-relative fitness comparison* rather than inferred conservation.

## Decisive contrasts, not just another morphological correlation

At a fixed floral architecture and matched flower age, compare two visitor functional types: one preferentially handling reward stamens and another foraging indiscriminately. For both, quantify **anther-class-specific pollen removal, grooming, body placement, stigma contact, pollen transfer to other flowers, and eventual fitness proxies**. Record morphology independently of function.

For the mechanical hypothesis, distinguish removing a long stamen in its entirety from selectively removing its anther while retaining a mechanically equivalent filament, and from a sham control. The 2026 *Bredia* manipulation shows why that contrast matters, but also why a direct reproductive fitness measure is essential before inferring maintenance.

For the evolutionary question, use populations with differing visitor communities and phylogenetic or repeated temporal comparisons to ask whether stamen dimorphism, role separation, and mechanical support move together or separately; account for population history and gene flow. A present-day pollinator effect does not itself demonstrate evolutionary selection for the anatomical state.

## Explicit SLK connection and claim ceiling

The focal SLK net architecture value `Phi = R-K` is **not a constant trait label**: the recoverable reward/transfer fitness and costs of an alternative architecture are conditional on pollinator behavior and extra functions supplied by existing organs. A two-function model can omit support/pose interactions and thus misidentify the biological reason morphology remains differentiated. This is an ecological hypothesis for how a trait may be **structurally retained while function is rewired**.

The published observations substantiate pieces (pollinator-specific handling, mechanical support, functional reassignment). **No study cited here directly identifies the full three-stage SLK threshold turnover, demonstrates long-term retained anatomy after breakdown of its original function, or estimates `Phi,g0,Delta_R` for the focal alternatives.** We do not claim new causal discovery of partner-dependent floral handling, mechanical support, or repurposing; those are already observed or theorized in the primary papers.

This is a stronger organismal *research direction*, not yet the missing full-SLK empirical confirmation. It may improve the flagship's natural-history motivation, but should not become a claim that the theory predicted those already published observations.
