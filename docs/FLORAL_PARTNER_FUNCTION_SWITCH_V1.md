# SLK empirical extension — pollinator-dependent function of differentiated stamens

## Ecological question

**Can a structurally divided floral organ remain differentiated after the ecological partner no longer uses its originally hypothesized roles — and could a second function retain that anatomy?**

This is a **proposed natural-history hypothesis**, not an observed evolutionary transition and not a new theorem. It asks whether a pollinator change can decouple (i) morphological stamen dimorphism, (ii) actual pollen-reward versus pollen-transfer specialization, and (iii) support of pollinator posture. These are different biological outcomes, not synonymous proxies for selection or fitness. A naturally repeated visitor, *Amegilla dulcifera*, acts as an anther-buzzing low-contribution visitor on *Melastoma candidum* (Hachiman et al. 2024) but a consistent flower-buzzing legitimate visitor on *Bredia hirsuta* (Fukano & Denda 2026). That contrast is across plant species, not a controlled floral transplant. Independent anther-architecture experiments already demonstrate pollinator-dependent ranking of pollen-transfer performance (Wilkins et al. 2022), so this interaction itself is prior art.

## A different biological outcome: partial erosion of heteranthery with altered pollen timing

Not every flower with an established pollen-feeding/pollen-export division retains it after ecological change. Across three matched large/small-flowered comparisons in *Solanum* section *Androceras*, smaller flowers repeatedly show reduced distinction between anther classes and reduced separation of anthers from stigmas, along with lower pollen:ovule ratios (Vallejo-Marín et al. 2014). Applying standardized bee-like vibrations also elicited faster pollen release from the small-flowered taxa (Kemp and Vallejo-Marín 2021). These observations place **structural de-specialization and modified temporal pollen release together** in a real comparative evolutionary system. The authors interpreted the structural syndrome as compatible with greater selfing, but did not establish a measured selfing-rate transition or identify pollinator change as its selective cause. Artificial vibrations reveal a pollen-dispensing phenotype, not natural pollen export, paternity or full fitness. The plants are not a direct test of the Bredia scaffold bypass: retention of mechanically useful filaments was not assayed. This is an already published biological result, **not a new SLK discovery or an estimate of `Phi`, `g0`, or `Delta_R`**.

### Experimental evidence for a reproductive-assurance route (2026)

Rubini Pisano and colleagues (2026, DOI 10.1093/aob/mcag186) revisited the same three *Solanum* section *Androceras* large/small-flowered comparisons with controlled pollination and floral phenotypic covariance analysis. **All three large-flowered taxa produced no fruit or seeds in the autonomous selfing treatment**, whereas the small-flowered *S. grayi* var. *grayi* (seed-autonomy index 0.02–0.13 across two populations) and *S. heterodoxum* (0.83–1.55) did. The third small-flowered *S. fructu-tecto* did not fruit under the tested greenhouse conditions, making its autonomous-selfing result **inconclusive**; it must not be counted as confirmed evidence against a functional shift. These ratios compare autonomous versus hand-selfed seed set and are **not field selfing rates**. Phenotypic covariances between corolla and reproductive traits relaxed, while some associations among sexual traits strengthened; covariance differences do **not** identify a genetic developmental constraint or SLK accessibility gradient.

**Autonomous pollen transfer is distinct from self-compatibility.** The same 2026 study detected high self-compatibility under manual self- versus cross-pollination in **all six taxa** (no species-level difference in seed/fruit set between manual self and manual outcross). However, the unassisted seed and fruit outcomes split with floral phenotype. The experimental index `AI_s` is seed set after autonomous selfing divided by seed set after **hand selfing**, and `AI_f` is the corresponding fruit-set ratio; neither is a natural selfing rate.

The authors interpret self-compatibility as an older condition in section *Androceras*, predating the three small-flower transitions (Rubini Pisano et al. 2026). This ancestral inference is **not directly dated by the pollination experiments**. The evidence makes a narrower distinction: reduced herkogamy and floral coordination may enable delivery of compatible pollen to a stigma that could already accept it. Morphological and pollen-delivery mechanisms, rather than the evolution of incompatibility alleles, are candidate explanations for the observed differences. Prior genetic studies give an outcrossing estimate for *S. rostratum* (reported `t=0.70–0.94` in Rubini Pisano et al. 2026), so the limitation is **no comparable field mating-system estimates across the matched taxa**, not that field mating-system information is absent for every species.

**The biological reallocation is incomplete, not an all-or-none loss of heteranthery.** In the original paired comparisons, the single pollinating anther held 50–67% of floral pollen in large-flowered taxa but still held 39–41% in the small-flowered taxa (Vallejo-Marín et al. 2014, restated in Rubini Pisano et al. 2026). Actual pollen fates from each anther class were not followed in the small-flowered taxa. Reduced morphological differentiation and reduced investment asymmetry therefore do not prove that a formerly specialized anther became reproductively unnecessary.

**Phenotypic integration is redirected rather than simply reduced.** The 2026 analysis reports weakening of covariation between feeding and pollinating anther traits, strengthening of selected anther–stigma covariances, and weakened covariation between corolla and sexual-organ traits. This can be consistent with greater coordination around autonomous fertilization while anther-to-anther specialism declines. It is an association pattern, not a measured evolutionary selection gradient, a heritable developmental constraint, or evidence that the pollen role conflict has disappeared. Even small flowers retain buzz-pollinated biology and potential outcross siring opportunities.

**Reproductive capability is not reproductive-assurance selection.** Selfed offspring can suffer inbreeding depression, and changes in autonomous selfing can lose outcross paternity through pollen discounting or replace seeds that would have been cross-fertilized (seed discounting). These are established features of mating-system evolution (Porcher & Lande 2005, DOI 10.1111/j.1420-9101.2005.00905.x). The controlled-cross comparison did not measure these full fitness trade-offs. Thus SLK should not claim that pollinator scarcity selected reduced heteranthery, or that anther-partition conflict necessarily weakens whenever autonomous selfing becomes possible.

This is a stronger organismal link than a selfing-syndrome inference alone: in two paired contrasts, structural reduction of heteranthery accompanies directly observed capacity for reproduction without pollinators. It does **not** prove that the reduction evolved *because* selfing increased or that the pollen-feeding-versus-export conflict remained constant. Relaxation of reliance on pollinators is a **competing route that can change the biological conflict itself**, distinct from keeping multifunctionality behind a fixed adaptive/path/establishment bottleneck.

## Three competing biological explanations for a retained stamen morphology

After pollinator change, suppose the large and small stamens are still visibly differentiated but pollen export between them becomes similar.

**H1: Retention by secondary functional support (proposed, not demonstrated).** Structural differentiation persists not because reward-versus-fertilization pollen specialization remains strong, but because stamen position/filament rigidity facilitates a reproducible pollinator grip, stigma contact, or another independent function. It predicts that targeted removal or altered stiffness of the supporting filaments impairs posture or realized pollen transfer **even after pollen-availability differences are experimentally controlled**. In repeated populations under different pollinator assemblages, this mechanical effect should predict relative reproductive success better than anther dimorphism alone. The 2026 *Bredia* evidence supports the existence of this mechanical role in one ecology; retention after a shift is not tested.

**H2: Ecological mismatch plus historical lag (proposed).** Morphology survives temporarily despite lower current fitness return, because the lineage has not experienced enough generations, mutational access is limited, or selection is weak. It predicts no independent mechanical benefit capable of offsetting the value lost from indiscriminate pollen collection, and potential subsequent erosion of stamen differentiation if these new environments persist. The 2024 *Senna* study demonstrates altered visitor handling but does not evaluate the transgenerational prediction.

**H3: New recruitment or de novo origin for temporal presentation (prior art).** Dimorphism provides differential removal timing of reproductive or food-body resources, rather than specialization between pollen reward and reproduction. Dellinger et al. (2021) inferred repeated **new origins** of heteranthery in passerine-pollinated lineages after pollen rewards were replaced; this is **not** evidence that ancestral heteranthery necessarily persisted through the transition. It predicts staggered removal through anthesis but does not by itself prove resulting outcross reproductive gains.

These scenarios are **not mutually exclusive**: mechanical support, staggered presentation, reward differentiation, and developmental inertia can coexist. A flower may preserve the same visible organ categories while functions and selective consequences change, but the proposed persistence mechanism needs a *candidate-relative fitness comparison* rather than inferred conservation.

## The missing competitor: keep the filament scaffold, reduce anther dimorphism

The *Bredia* intervention sharpened the evolutionary hypothesis. **Retention of a mechanically useful long-stamen filament is not the same as retention of a specialized pollen-bearing long anther.** In treatment D, long anthers were removed while their filaments remained, and buzzing stayed stable (0 slipping events in 31 visits). That flower was surgically manipulated, **not** an evolved scaffold-preserving isomorphic type; its pollen and lifetime reproductive performance were not established. But the result means that an evolutionary model cannot simply assume that reducing anther differentiation necessarily destroys all mechanical support.

A third candidate architecture must be considered alongside the intact differentiated flower and a completely unsupported simplified one: a **reduced-anther-dimorphism flower retaining equivalent mechanical support**. This candidate may be less costly, equivalent in mechanics, developmentally unavailable, or worse in reproductive success. All remain empirical questions.

In the explicit conditional four-architecture illustration in `theory/FLORAL_SCAFFOLD_BYPASS_V1.md`, `pi_z` denotes residual pollen-role benefit, `mu_z` support benefit, `gamma_z` their additional interaction, and `c_a` the extra cost of maintaining differentiated pollen-bearing anthers. The complete differentiated flower beats the scaffold-preserving reduced-anther alternative only when

```text
pi_z + gamma_z > c_a.
```

**The mechanical benefit `mu_z` cancels from this direct comparison.** If the original pollen-partitioning advantage vanishes and an independently useful scaffold could be retained cheaply, mechanical support may select for **filament preservation but against anther dimorphism**. If pollen differentiation provides an additional reproductive advantage *because* a scaffold is present, a positive `gamma_z` could instead favor keeping both. The published Bredia manipulation measures **neither** of these complete-fitness comparisons, nor does it show that the scaffold-preserving alternative is evolutionarily accessible.

This creates a biologically consequential difference between H1 (*selective retention of the complete differentiated floral architecture*) and H2 (*historical persistence while a mechanically functional simplified architecture is not available*). It can be tested using anther differentiation and support manipulations that leave pollen supply comparable, plus male and female reproductive endpoints under alternative visitor communities. Behavior during buzzing alone cannot identify `gamma_z`.

## Decisive contrasts, not just another morphological correlation

At a fixed floral architecture and matched flower age, compare two visitor functional types: one preferentially handling reward stamens and another foraging indiscriminately. For both, quantify **anther-class-specific pollen removal, grooming, body placement, stigma contact, pollen transfer to other flowers, and eventual fitness proxies**. Record morphology independently of function.

For the mechanical hypothesis, distinguish removing a long stamen in its entirety from selectively removing its anther while retaining a mechanically equivalent filament, and from a sham control. The 2026 *Bredia* manipulation directly shows effects of long-stamen structures on buzz stability, but **long stamens alone without short stamens also supported stable buzzing (0 slips in 26 visits)**, so it does not establish that both classes are mechanically indispensable. Nor do visit-level event counts establish lifetime reproductive selection or the evolutionary maintenance of dimorphism.

For the evolutionary question, use populations with differing visitor communities and phylogenetic or repeated temporal comparisons to ask whether stamen dimorphism, role separation, and mechanical support move together or separately; account for population history and gene flow. A present-day pollinator effect does not itself demonstrate evolutionary selection for the anatomical state.

## Explicit SLK connection and claim ceiling

The focal SLK net architecture value `Phi = R-K` is **not a constant trait label**: the recoverable reward/transfer fitness and costs of an alternative architecture are conditional on pollinator behavior and extra functions supplied by existing organs. A two-function model can omit support/pose interactions and thus misidentify the biological reason morphology remains differentiated. This is an ecological hypothesis for how a trait may be **structurally retained while function is rewired**.

The published observations substantiate pieces (pollinator-specific handling, mechanical support, functional reassignment). **No study cited here directly identifies the full three-stage SLK threshold turnover, demonstrates long-term retained anatomy after breakdown of its original function, or estimates `Phi,g0,Delta_R` for the focal alternatives.** We do not claim new causal discovery of partner-dependent floral handling, mechanical support, or repurposing; those are already observed or theorized in the primary papers.

This is a stronger organismal *research direction*, not yet the missing full-SLK empirical confirmation. It may improve the flagship's natural-history motivation, but should not become a claim that the theory predicted those already published observations.
