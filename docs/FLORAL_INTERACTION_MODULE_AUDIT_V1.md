# SLK: floral organ function is a trait of plant–visitor interactions, not an anther label

## Biological result of auditing the primary observations

A differentiated stamen can provide **pollen food, export pollen, transfer vibration, stabilize bee grip, and affect attraction**. A two-class morphology does not imply a one-to-one mapping from stamen class to pollen fate. Nor does loss of one function demonstrate structural degeneration: its other components can remain involved in the mechanics of pollination. These statements are directly grounded in manipulations and behavioral contrasts, **but no cited study establishes long-term evolutionary retention because another function replaced a lost one**.

This audit corrects three common conflations:

1. **Visit/posture versus reproductive function.** Stigma-facing positioning and fluorescence applied to a *stigma* identify the bee region contacting that stigma. They do **not** establish the paternal destination of pollen released from each anther class. Direct anther-specific labeling and outcross pollen-transfer/fertility estimates are still needed to attribute male fitness.
2. **Mechanical effect versus a fitness-selected maintenance mechanism.** Removing a stamen can change buzzing, arrival, exit or visual reward. Changes in slipping and stigma contact directly establish effects on behavior, not whether selection historically preserved anther dimorphism.
3. **Retention versus repeated origin.** Comparative occurrence of a structure under two ecological regimes is compatible with phylogenetic maintenance, re-evolution, or independent de novo origin. These are different evolutionary histories.

## A naturally repeated visitor, different ecological role

An unusually strong *natural* comparison is already present across two Okinawan Melastomataceae studies:

| Same bee species | Floral context | Observed contact/behavior | Permissible inference |
|---|---|---|---|
| *Amegilla dulcifera* on *Melastoma candidum* (Hachiman, Uejo & Denda 2024) | Larger flowers with differentiated stamens | Buzzes one anther or a few anthers, with limited stigma contact/pollination reported for this bee; *Xylocopa flavifrons* is the principal effective visitor | A bee's functional role is not fixed by its species identity |
| *Amegilla dulcifera* on *Bredia hirsuta* (Fukano & Denda 2026) | Small flowers with closely spaced differentiated stamens | Buzzes the flower while gripping short stamens and long-stamen filaments, with consistent dorsal stigma contact | Flower geometry and grip configuration plausibly change *the same bee's* observed role from anther-buzzing visitor with limited stigma contact to consistent stigma-contacting flower buzzer |

This is an **observational cross-plant contrast**, not a reciprocal transplant of a bee among randomized floral architectures. Unmeasured effects of floral rewards, phenology, bee condition, community context or flower identity prevent assigning the change exclusively to flower size or stamen position.

Primary sources:
- Hachiman S, Uejo M, Denda T. 2024. Division of labour between dimorphic stamens in *Melastoma candidum* (Melastomataceae): role of stamen strength in the biomechanics of pollination. *Journal of Pollination Ecology* 37:284–302. DOI 10.26786/1920-7603(2024)810.
- Fukano T, Denda T. 2026. Consistent buzz-pollination posture suggests division of labour between dimorphic stamens in *Bredia hirsuta* (Melastomataceae) on Okinawa-Jima Island, Japan. *Plant Species Biology* 41:e70070. DOI 10.1111/1442-1984.70070.

## Bredia: component-removal contrasts map behavior without proving ancestral specialization

The 2026 paper compared six selected combinations of **short stamens** (S), **long-stamen filaments** (LF), and **long-stamen anthers** (LA); it did not run a complete three-factor factorial experiment. The experimentally observed event counts are:

| Treatment | S | LF | LA | Visits | Slips during buzzing | Fall at departure | Ventral stigma contact |
|---|:---:|:---:|:---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| A: all intact | + | + | + | 33 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| B: only short | + | – | – | 39 | 12 | 1 | 13 |
| C: only entire long | – | + | + | 26 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| D: long anthers removed | + | + | – | 31 | 0 | 2 | 0 |
| E: long filaments only | – | + | – | 2 | 1 | 0 | 0 |
| F: all stamens removed | – | – | – | 0 | n/a | n/a | n/a |

Source: Fukano & Denda 2026, Table 1 and text of Results §§3.3–3.4.

**Strong inference:** treatment B shows that a short-stamen-only flower permits many visits yet a different, unstable buzzing posture; keeping long filaments (A/D) or complete long stamens (C) is associated with no recorded slipping, consistent with mechanical support supplied by long-stamen structures. Long-stamen filaments were thicker than short filaments (reported means 672.9 versus 429.3 μm). **Do not infer** that both stamen categories are mechanically essential: treatment C (long stamens present without short stamens) also had 0/26 slips. Treatment E had only two visits; it cannot establish a precise rate or prove a general mechanical synergy. Nor is a count of 12 slips out of 39 visits equivalent to 39 statistically independent flowers: the same flower was visited repeatedly, so visit-level proportions are descriptive.

**Second independent function:** treatment E had much lower visitation than treatments A–D, indicating that removal of short-anther visual cues or rewards can affect *attraction*, not merely mechanical grip. In D, long anthers were removed but long filaments and short stamens remained; buzzing was stable yet departure falls were observed (2/31). Therefore **stable buzzing is not identical to secure departure**: different components may contribute at different phases of a single pollinator visit.

The four candidate floral functions must be treated separately: (i) attracting the bee, (ii) supporting buzzing, (iii) exporting pollen to an outcross mate, and (iv) permitting safe exit/stigma contact. Loss of one does not logically imply loss of all.

## Senna and Solanum: what the experimental interventions actually establish

- Rego, Monzón & Mesquita-Neto (2024, DOI 10.1111/plb.13673) surveyed three *Senna arnottiana* populations; large native bees tended to manipulate short feeding anthers and introduced *Bombus terrestris* used both classes indiscriminately and contacted the stigma less often. **Partner-dependent disruption of the usual feeding/pollinating division is directly observed**; measured visitor behavior does not demonstrate long-term selection to maintain or erase dimorphism.
- Wilkins, Mayberry, Vallejo-Marín & Russell (2022, DOI 10.1007/s00442-022-05246-0) manipulated free versus joined anthers within *Solanum elaeagnifolium*, with pollen transfer to stigmas by large versus small *Bombus impatiens* switching which architecture performs better. The 2023 journal correction, DOI 10.1007/s00442-023-05413-x, corrects **Figure 1's legend**, not the paper's stated architecture-by-body-size result. This is a **manipulated functional payoff** not a reproduction-level estimate of an evolving heterantherous alternative.

The novel work for SLK is not to rediscover anatomy-by-pollinator interactions, but to ask whether the **fitness effect of retaining versus reducing a specialized structural module** depends on *which* of its functional roles is currently useful.

## Merianieae: a critical evolutionary-history distinction

Dellinger et al. (2021, DOI 10.1111/evo.14260) used 63 Merianieae species and ancestral reconstruction. Their account is specifically that heteranthery has **often arisen de novo in passerine-pollinated, food-body-rewarding lineages after shifts from pollen reward**, rather than being primarily an ancestral heteranthery that simply remained in place following loss of the pollen dilemma. The paper estimated approximately 7.29 isomorphic-to-heterantherous and 4.05 reverse transitions on its reconstruction (model-average values, **not literal observed events**), and itself stresses alternative stamen roles such as staggered nutrient presentation.

**Consequently:** it is evidence for **alternative functional origins** of the same differentiated architecture, not direct support for anatomical persistence driven by a secondary function. If SLK's proposed evolutionary-retention hypothesis is to be established, a lineage must actually start with a differentiated structure that pre-exists the partner change, retain it across that change, and show a fitness benefit from another role relative to accessible reduced-dimorphism alternatives. Merianieae does not currently satisfy those three conditions as a general rule.

## Nontrivial testable biological claim, carefully conditional

A floral architecture can be **functionally modular for one output and mechanically integrated for another**. For example, short and long stamens may differentially partition pollen consumption versus export while together contributing to body positioning and visit dynamics. This is **multiple interacting functions of one morphological module**, rather than an automatic one-trait/one-function specialization.

To discriminate retention from independent origin or evolutionary lag, compare within a lineage or repeatedly matched relatives: (a) historic/ancestral architecture, (b) visitor functional change, (c) stamen-class-specific pollen export and complete reproductive fitness under both visitor regimes, and (d) independently manipulated mechanical versus pollen-role contributions. Where the relevant differentiated organ is already known to pre-date the change, a positive selective contribution from the new mechanical/timing role despite diminished former value supports *secondary-function retention*. If differentiation evolved only after the partner shift, the mechanism is instead **de novo functional recruitment**, not evolutionary memory or retention. If the structure is no longer beneficial and no alternative benefit offsets cost, observed short-term persistence is compatible with lag or developmental constraint.

### Claim ceiling

No original SLK data were generated. The cross-plant *Amegilla* contrast is observational. *Bredia* treatment proportions cannot be interpreted as independent whole-plant fitness replicates. Stigma powder is not an anther-specific paternity test. Merianieae repeated de novo origins do not demonstrate retention. The full ordered value/path/rare-invasion SLK trajectory in one natural species is still untested. These boundaries should remain in the flagship theory manuscript and evidence ledger.
