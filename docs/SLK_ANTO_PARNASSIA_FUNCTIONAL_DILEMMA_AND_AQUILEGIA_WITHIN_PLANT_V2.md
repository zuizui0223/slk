# SLK × ANTO: an actual staminode mutualist–antagonist dilemma, and a separate incomplete organ-loss series

**2026-10-21 | Biological evidence synthesis, not a new floral manipulation, QTL reanalysis or general theorem.** This source review distinguishes three complementary published organisms and explicitly prevents stitching them into one fabricated empirical causal chain.

## Main biological question

A stamen-derived organ loses its ancestral pollen-production function, but can acquire support, deception, contact manipulation or herbivore-decoy roles. **Can ecological interactions maintain a modified organ despite conflict, and does the retained developmental architecture leave routes for later modification or recovery?**

SLK addresses *payoff and architectural rearrangement under partner interactions*. ANTO addresses *which parts of a lost organ can remain developmentally available and how they can reappear*. Both must refer to the SAME organ and SAME lineages to constitute a strong joint empirical test. That comprehensive study has **not** been performed in any of the sources below.

## 1. Parnassia: controlled field evidence for a real attract–consume dilemma

Wen, Chen, Rech et al. (2024), *Ecology and Evolution* 14:e70380, DOI **10.1002/ece3.70380**, studied nectar-mimicking glossy staminodes in the subalpine species *Parnassia wightiana* (Celastraceae). The study uses **the same organ** in interaction with prospective pollinators (bees/flies) and the florivorous chrysomelid beetle *Nonarthra variabilis*.

**Original, directly reported manipulations and observations:**

- Staminode removal reduced prospective pollinator visits and visit duration (authors reported p<0.001). In both 2013 and 2014, clipping only the glossy staminode tips reduced mature seeds per fruit relative to intact flowers (p<0.001). These are genuine author experimental outcomes, NOT our independent re-estimation of a site-adjusted effect size.
- 2013: 170 flowers tagged; 64 intact-control, 30 whole-staminode-removed and 44 apex-clipped fruits were subsequently collected (138 collected total; attrition from cattle damage reported). 2014 recovered 40/36/42 fruits in the respective groups. Unequal survival/recovery and choice of site must not be silently treated as randomized balanced retained observations.
- For visitation rate the authors paired 80 flowering plants (40 pairs) in 2023 at site B (date as reported in Methods, while earlier clipping/seed experiments concern 2013/2014); each pair had six 10-minute visits sampled (60 minutes per pair). The 2014 visit-duration sample comprised 47 control visits versus 27 on staminode-removed flowers.
- In natural florivory observations, beetles first chewed the glossy tips, then whole staminodes, later petal fringes/stamens, rarely contacting ovaries/stigmas. In site A, roughly **70%** of inspected flowers had tip-chewing and **58%** lacked the staminodes owing to chewing; these site counts are descriptive and not independent selection gradients.
- In feeding-choice assays the authors ran **405 arena trials** over multiple floral-part pairings (35–42 replicates per treatment). Beetles favored staminodes over petals or ovaries but did NOT invariably prefer staminodes over petal fringes or whole stamens. Other animals' avoidance of beetle-occupied flowers was observed, but the causal number of pollen grains/achene failures mediated specifically by that avoidance was not quantified with a full factorial exclusion experiment.

### The SLK result this really bears on

**Observed:** A highly modified ancestral stamen-derived structure promotes floral visitation/seed production under some conditions, yet also invites consumption by an antagonistic visitor. Different components of the *same* organ (glossy tip versus whole ring) are experimentally relevant.

**Not observed:** The net fitness selection gradient on gloss area independent of organ shape, an ecologically calibrated separation R and K, a naturally evolved specialized decoy appendage, evolutionary rarity advantage, genetically correlated alternatives or proven sacrificial ovary shielding. The ovary-sparing role is an attractive **hypothesis**: preferential consumption of staminodes over ovaries does not by itself prove the organ reduces the counterfactual ovary damage or increases total viable recruitment. Damage-induced scents after clipping, floral stage, and differently sampled sites/year are also potential confounders.

### Critical data-access limitation

The authors report a full dataset in Dryad **10.5061/dryad.b2rbnzsqg**. We attempted to resolve the dataset page (not found), and the public Dryad API returned `Identifier cannot be viewed. Either you lack permission to view it, or it is missing required elements.` (dataset internal ID 139500). The publication's shared data link also did not resolve. Therefore **no raw Dryad reanalysis, new seed effect sizes, or independent full manipulation replication is claimed here**. Evidence above comes from the published article and published experimental descriptions.

**A decisive evolutionary-ecology contrast:** repeat a deliberately matched *2 × 2* ecological experiment (functional staminode tip present vs absent/sham-controlled; florivorous beetle access present vs excluded), measure simultaneous pollinator deposition, ovary damage, seed set and offspring emergence while following full flowering cohorts. A protective-decoy hypothesis requires **an interaction** on actual seed/ovule damage or recruitment, not just a beetle preference score. Experiments must be newly permitted, with actual lineage/cross-origin IDs and interspecific extrapolation controlled. This is a design proposal, not an authorized activity.

## 2. Penstemon: actual alternative pollination functions of the derivative organ

Walker-Larsen & Harder (2001), *Evolution* 55:477–487, DOI **10.1111/j.0014-3820.2001.tb00782.x**, removed sterile staminodes in two bee-pollinated and two hummingbird-pollinated *Penstemon* species.

- *P. ellipticus*: staminode as a nectar-access obstruction that lengthens handling and promotes stigma contact/pollen receipt.
- *P. palmeri*: staminode acts as a lever promoting stigma contact without documented increase in visit duration or pollen removal.
- *P. centranthifolius* and *P. rostriflorus*: the published experiment did not detect an effect of removal on its measured pollination components.

This confirms **functionally different outcomes of a homologous organ class**, not a common mechanically identical lever, not quantified total reproductive fitness, and not contemporary individual-level molecular proof of ancestral native regain. The origin, loss and reappearance of staminodes were previously phylogenetically inferred by the authors. Vestigial-organ functional reinvention is therefore **prior art**, not a novel claim invented by SLK × ANTO.

## 3. Aquilegia: original F2 reveals coupled yet not entirely synchronous organ identity

Johns, Min, Ballerini, Kramer & Hodges (2024), *EvoDevo* 15:6, DOI **10.1186/s13227-024-00225-3**; source is the authors' original Supplement 3 F2 morphology, copied with attribution and audited in [SLK PR #121](https://github.com/zuizui0223/slk/pull/121). The paper reports **nine** independent QTL and overlapping *and* whorl-specific trait intervals, explaining **44–53%** of quantitative trait variation across trait models. The original source CSV has NO genotype calls, so those genetics are not refitted here.

There were **231** phenotyped F2 plants, **400 flowers**, and **800** paired inner/outer whorl trait scores. Stamen versus staminode identity is measured as anther present/absent (AN), filament lateral expansion 0–3 (LE), and inter-whorl fusion (FU). Anther presence does NOT identify pollen viability. FU is scored once biologically per flower and duplicated on both whorl rows.

**One-generation morphological access:** 187/800 whorl rows exhibit anther present **with** LE≥2 **and** interwhorl FU=1; these occur in 129/400 flowers and 91/231 plants. 99/400 flowers carry outer anthers while inner anthers are absent, and 0 show the reverse. Only two of 400 show outer LE greater than inner; the original authors used one randomly selected flower per F2 plant to compare whorls, a **different denominator**.

**New explicit same-plant readout from repeated flowers** (169 plants with both flowers labeled a and b): 38 plants show that AN+LE+FU mosaic phenotype in BOTH flowers, 23 only in flower a, 16 only in flower b, and 92 in neither. Scoring **only flower a** detects it in **61/169 = 36.1%**, whereas scoring **either** measured flower detects it in **77/169 = 45.6%**. Of the 108 individuals without the phenotype in flower a, 16 (14.8%) show it in flower b. Same-genotype AN and FU remain relatively stable across flowers (AN both whorls identical in 119/169; FU identical in 154/169), demonstrating **partial developmental penetrance, not unconstrained organ reassembly**. These source-grain values do not imply the individual genetically lost and then naturally regained the program; they show an observation-based failure to see morphology in one flower cannot certify developmental inability in that same F2 plant.

An explicit source discrepancy is preserved rather than hidden: direct Supplement 3 paired flowering data yield **138/169** exactly equal inner LE scores; the 2024 paper's prose reports **139/169**. Published morphotype summaries use subtly different one-flower-per-plant sampling for some analyses. Neither this one-count difference nor the two outer-greater F2 flower scores overturn authors' QTL/genetic conclusions. Both require reconciliation if the source is to be used as a new analytical cohort.

**Important ecological distinction:** the Aquilegia staminode sheath persists around developing carpels after outer floral organs fall. Lignification and enriched defense-associated transcripts suggest an *ovary/fruit protection function*, but **that functional benefit was not experimentally verified in this F2 CSV**. This is NOT the pollinator contact lever tested in Penstemon or the glossy nectar-mimic attractor tested in Parnassia.

## 4. The actual SLK × ANTO question and where evidence genuinely ends

| Empirical contribution | Positive biological result | Unsupported leap |
|---|---|---|
| Parnassia | One modified organ influences visits, seeds and attracts florivores | Its removal's net evolutionary threshold or trait-afterlife rate |
| Penstemon | The derived sterile stamen can help pollinators via different modes, or show no detectable benefit in birds | Same ancestral program genetically returned at a measured frequency |
| Aquilegia | Same F2 genotype can show different mosaic organ states across flowers; related subtraits have directional whorl constraints | Wild lineage naturally restored function, or independent functional genetic modules proven |

**The integrated prediction worth falsifying is narrower than prior art**:

> Among lineages with documented ancestral staminode loss, partial developmental retention of the ancestral organ can allow a pollination, visual-attraction or post-anthesis protective **derived** role to persist without restoring ancestral gamete production, but whether that capacity persists should depend on the ecological benefits/costs of retaining the organ and whether intact genetic modules can still re-express the same structure.

This needs *same-lineage* genetic resources, development, actual functional effect on both interacting guilds, and descendant persistence. None of the three studies measures all of those. Together they make that design biologically motivated, NOT validated.

If choosing a target empirical system **now**, Parnassia is strongest for the SLK conflict (mutualists and antagonists on one structure); Aquilegia is strongest for ANTO's observable architectural reassembly; Penstemon is the direct precedent for functional reinvention. Do not pretend a three-taxon assemblage is one matched evolutionary experiment.

### A same-species molecular reference exists, but it is not the missing staminode assay

Tang et al. (published online 28 November 2025, *BMC Plant Biology* 26:4, 2026), DOI [10.1186/s12870-025-07646-9](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12870-025-07646-9), additionally studied **Parnassia wightiana** using whole-tissue insecticidal extracts, metabolites, and **shoot/flower** RNA-seq (GSA accession **CRA028314** reported in the published Data Availability section). The authors report 87 TPS-family members in their annotated transcriptomic study; roots were excluded from RNA-seq because of extraction challenges, although their root extracts were highest in insecticidal activity against *Mythimna separata* larvae.

This is useful as a potential *species-level* sequence resource to identify candidate genes and tissues in a later organ-specific study. **It is neither the staminode-specific expression nor the same plant genotypes in Wen et al. 2024**; RNA of whole flowers and shoots does not localize a retained native functional program to staminode apices, and insect toxicity toward moth larvae does not prove defense against the florivorous beetle *Nonarthra*. We did NOT download/reanalyse this new RNA dataset, and its GSA accession is a published source claim, not a verified local sequence inventory.

The best falsifiable *within-species* ecological question now becomes whether glossy staminodes attract beetles but also redirect them away from more consequential female tissues, and whether biochemical/structural differences among staminodes, filaments and ovaries change the actual ovule-level fitness cost. This requires organ-specific chemistry/transcriptomes and a beetle × floral-organ factorial experiment on matched identifiable plants. **Do not equate insecticidal compound abundance in roots with ancestral stamen developmental retention.**
